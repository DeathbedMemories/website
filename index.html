Why "Deathbed Memories"

The name isn't morbid — it's a filter. It's the question I run every trip through before I book a single thing.

The idea

Most people plan travel around what's convenient, popular, or safe to post. I plan around what you'll actually remember when it counts — near the end of your life, looking back. Deathbed Memories is what's left after you strip away the itinerary filler: the moments worth having been there for.

The practice

I built this approach on the road — four months of daily travel across seven countries, documented in real time. Not a highlight reel shot after the fact, but the actual decision-making: why I booked what I booked, what a local told me that changed my plans, what I'd do differently.

The promise

When it's time to feel ready — whenever that comes — I want you to be able to say you packed light, stayed a while, lived like a local, and walked the paths most people skip. That's the trip I plan for you.

How I plan travel

Every itinerary I build gets run through the same five values. If these match how you want to travel, we'll work well together.

  • Local over tourist Fewer landmarks, more of what the people who actually live there do on a normal Tuesday.
  • Slower travel Stay at least a week before moving on. Depth over a checklist of cities.
  • Sustainable approach Travel that doesn't burn you — or the places you're visiting — out.
  • Safety, seriously Especially as a solo traveler myself, safety isn't an afterthought in any plan I build.
  • Affordability A great trip and a realistic budget aren't opposites. I plan for both.

Who this is for: aspiring digital nomads figuring out remote work abroad, solo adventurers who want to explore on foot, travelers tired of touristy itineraries, and burned-out professionals ready for a real change of pace.
